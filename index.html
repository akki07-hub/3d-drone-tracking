Case Study & Research

Problem Statement

Indoor drone tracking is challenging because GPS signals are weak, highly attenuated, or completely unavailable inside buildings due to concrete and steel structures. This creates a reliance on alternative indoor positioning systems.

Objective

To accurately track a drone's 3D position and movement in real-time by combining multiple sensor modalities to compensate for individual sensor weaknesses.

Proposed Solution

A multimodal tracking framework leveraging:

  • Camera Imagery: For visual odometry and feature tracking.
  • Depth/LiDAR Data: For precise distance measurement and 3D point cloud generation.
  • IMU Measurements: For high-frequency acceleration and angular velocity readings to track rapid movements.

Methodology

Sensor acquisition → Preprocessing → Feature extraction → Sensor fusion (e.g., Extended Kalman Filter) → Position estimation → Visualization.

Applications

Warehouses, industrial inspection, indoor navigation, search and rescue operations, and automated inventory management.

Challenges

Occlusion, rapid lighting changes, sensor noise, IMU drift over time, and obstacle avoidance in cluttered environments.

Evaluation Metrics

Position error (RMSE), tracking stability, latency, and obstacle detection accuracy.

Limitations and Future Scope

Limitations include computational constraints for real-time processing on edge devices. Future scope includes real hardware integration, full Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM), visual-inertial odometry (VIO), and AI-based object detection for dynamic obstacle avoidance.

Why Sensor Fusion?

Individual sensors have flaws. Cameras struggle in low light or textureless walls. LiDAR can be confused by glass or highly reflective surfaces. IMUs suffer from rapid drift due to double integration of acceleration. Fusing these modalities (e.g., using an Extended Kalman Filter) allows the system to cross-validate measurements and maintain accurate 3D tracking even if one sensor temporarily fails.
